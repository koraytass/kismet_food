# Stitch Design Analysis

This note defines the direction for the Kismet Foods redesign. The goal is not to copy Stitch screens, but to keep the same design discipline across every page.

## What Works

- One continuous editorial canvas: sections feel connected through spacing, image rhythm, and thin rules instead of isolated cards.
- Strong type hierarchy: one large serif idea per viewport, supported by restrained mono labels.
- Image-led composition: product and origin visuals carry the mood; text sits beside or over them with clear restraint.
- Repeated small primitives: eyebrow rule, italic accent word, hairline dividers, compact CTA links.
- Asymmetry with purpose: featured content gets scale; secondary content becomes a ledger/list, not another card.

## What To Avoid

- Reusing the same hero and same two-card layout on every page.
- Adding logos everywhere as decoration.
- Generic metrics such as `24h`, `Day 1`, `Day 2`, or duplicate day labels when the nearby item already explains timing.
- Heavy boxed UI unless the box represents a real object: product, origin, report, process step, or form.
- Random dark sections. Dark backgrounds should be reserved for photographic product moments or true contrast.

## Page System

- Home: cinematic editorial flow, product teaser, origin bridge, short operational proof.
- Products: image-led portfolio atlas with asymmetric product cards.
- Product detail: technical product desk, spec/grade/origin ledger, quote path.
- Origins: interactive map first, then country/product origin rows.
- Supply chain: process and logistics ledger, no repeated day labels.
- About: company method and credibility narrative, less decoration.
- Insights: report list and market desk structure.
- Legal/contact: quiet document/form layouts.

## Brand Use

- Full logo: header, footer, and one hero lockup only.
- Teal/orange mark: tiny punctuation or section signature, not repeated wallpaper.
- Colors: paper base, night text, teal/orange accents. Let images provide richness.
